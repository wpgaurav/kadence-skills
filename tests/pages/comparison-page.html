The bike is slower between drops and much faster at them. Parking and walking to the door is where the van loses: a median of 4 minutes 20 seconds per stop against 40 seconds on the bike. Over sixty drops that is nearly four hours.

Rain costs the bike about 12% of its round in winter, and there is no version of this where a bike moves a pallet. Two of our four depots went back to vans for the outer suburbs after a year, and we think that was the right call.

What the riders and drivers said

Method, in full

Costs are fully loaded and include depreciation on a four-year replacement cycle, insurance, energy, maintenance parts and labour, depot space apportioned by footprint, and the rider or driver at the rate we actually pay including employer contributions.

Rounds were assigned by the same routing software throughout, with no manual reassignment, which is the main reason we trust the comparison. It also means neither vehicle got a round tuned to its strengths, so both numbers are probably a little worse than a well-planned operation would see.

Two things we did not measure and should have: rider turnover against driver turnover, and the effect of the bikes on complaint rates. Both were raised by depot managers and neither has clean data behind it, so we have left them out rather than guess.
